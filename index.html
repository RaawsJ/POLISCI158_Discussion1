Project interest

How effectively is the U.S. Army integrating artificial intelligence across echelons, and what operational benefits, institutional costs, and adoption barriers shape the gap between Soldier use and civilian use of generative AI?

Policy questions

  • What benefits, risks, and organizational tradeoffs has AI integration created for the U.S. Army, and which policies would enable responsible adoption at scale?
  • How does AI adoption vary among junior enlisted Soldiers, NCOs, warrant officers, company-grade officers, and field-grade officers, and how do those patterns compare with U.S. civilian adoption?
  • To what extent do security restrictions, approved-tool availability, digital literacy, leadership climate, and perceived usefulness explain differences in generative-AI adoption across Army echelons relative to the U.S. public?

Study boundaries

**AI adoption** has two distinct meanings in this study:

  • Institutional adoption: The Army fields AI-enabled systems for intelligence, logistics, maintenance, training, targeting, and administration.
  • Individual adoption: Soldiers personally use approved, or potentially unapproved, AI tools for work tasks, education, writing, coding, analysis, or planning.
